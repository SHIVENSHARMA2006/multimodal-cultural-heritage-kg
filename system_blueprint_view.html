SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE TECHNICAL SUMMARY & PARADIGM FORMULATION

1.1 The Engineering Problem: Preserving Contested Low-Resource Heritage

Contemporary Natural Language Processing (NLP), Knowledge Graph (KG) construction, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) paradigms operate under two implicit, highly damaging assumptions when applied to low-resource indigenous cultural heritage:

  • 1. Orthographic and Textual Chauvinism: Mainstream pipelines require massive monolingual digitized corpora. Low-resource heritage languages (such as Manipuri / Meitei) exist primarily in archaic scripts (Meetei Mayek), physically deteriorated bark manuscripts (Puyas), sacred architecture, and unwritten oral traditions. Standard pipelines treat oral histories as second-class noise and remain blind to non-textual evidence modalities.
  • 2. The Canonical Truth Monoculture: Standard entity resolution, knowledge fusion, and KG completion algorithms enforce a single canonical ground truth via majority voting or source-authority weighting. In post-colonial cultural contexts, high-resource colonial records (such as British military gazetteers from 1891) directly contradict indigenous royal chronicles (Cheitharol Kumbaba) and living oral narratives. Enforcing a single truth silently overwrites indigenous historical memory, producing culturally destructive hallucinations.

1.2 The Core Novel Paradigm: Epistemic Pluralism via CIDOC-CRM E13

To solve this systemic failure, this system formalizes the paradigm of Epistemic Pluralism in Knowledge Engineering. Instead of arbitrating disputes, the system treats conflicting historical claims as first-class, co-equal data entities. Grounded in the international museum standard CIDOC Conceptual Reference Model (CIDOC-CRM ISO 21127), every property assertion is modeled as an E13 Attribute Assignment event rather than a static edge.

An E13 Attribute Assignment explicitly reifies: (1) the source literature or elder testimony, (2) the evidence modality (HTR text, VLM image feature, ASR speech chunk), (3) the source reliability tier (Tier A: government/royal chronicle, Tier B: peer-reviewed research, Tier C: tertiary blogs), (4) the exact timestamp/epoch, and (5) the divergence classification (None, Soft, Hard).

SECTION 5: INGESTION SUBSYSTEM (THE FOUR MODALITIES)

The ingestion subsystem handles the acquisition, digitization, script parsing, and feature extraction across four distinct historical modalities.

5.1 Modality 1: Meetei Mayek Manuscript HTR (ingestion/htr_module.py)

Historical manuscripts in Manipur are preserved as Puyas—handwritten texts on agarwood bark (Aquilaria agallocha) inscribed in ancient Meetei Mayek. The HTR module transcribes high-resolution manuscript scans into Unicode text and Latin transliteration.

  • 1. Anatomical Letter Mapping: Traditional Meetei Mayek consists of 27 primary characters (Iyek 27), each named after a human anatomical body part (Kok = Head, Sam = Hair, Mit = Eye, Pa = Eyelash, Na = Ear, etc.). The module maintains an exact phonetic and anatomical dictionary mapping each glyph to its sound, meaning, and Unicode hex code.
  • 2. Confidence Calculation: Every transcribed folio undergoes stroke-density analysis and OCR feature extraction, yielding a character-level and folio-level confidence score (ranging between 0.88 and 0.96 for historical folios).
  • 3. Corpus Assets Ingested: The catalog contains 16th-century Subika illustrated astrology manuscripts and Chainarol warrior chronicles.

5.2 Modality 2: Monument Photography & VLM Iconography (ingestion/vlm_caption_module.py)

The Vision-Language Model (VLM) module processes architectural photography of sacred monuments and ruins across Kangleipak:

  • 1. Architectural Element Tagging: Detects corbelled arches, brick citadel bastions, sacred dragon iconography (Kangla Sha), floral terracotta carvings, and imperial gateways.
  • 2. Materiality & Condition Extraction: Identifies historical construction materials (calcified brick masonry, timber structural posts, gilded copper canopies) and structural condition.
  • 3. CIDOC-CRM Candidate Triples: Generates formal candidate triples asserting relations such as `P46_is_composed_of` (e.g., 'Kangla Sha Statues' -> composed_of -> 'Burned Brick & Lime Plaster').

5.3 Modality 3: Consented Oral Recordings & ASR (ingestion/asr_module.py)

Oral narratives (Funga Wari, ritual hymns, elder testimonies) represent the lifeblood of living Meetei culture. The ASR module transcribes spoken Manipuri audio with rigorous consent tracking:

  • 1. CARE Principles Enforcement: Audio files cannot be queried or ingested without explicit metadata verifying speaker consent, elder clan affiliation, recording date, and sovereign access tier.
  • 2. 3-Tier Transcripts: Produces: (a) Spoken Manipuri transcript, (b) Phonetic Latin transliteration, and (c) Grounded English translation.
  • 3. Oral Heritage Catalog: Houses consented elder testimonies on Kangla sacred origins, Lai Haraoba ritual chants, Sankirtana rhythmic cycles (Khol/Kartal), and traditional ecological folk tales (Tapta, Keibu Keioiba).

5.4 Modality 4: Archival Text Normalization (ingestion/text_normalize.py)

The text normalization engine unifies heterogeneous written materials across three orthographic regimes:

  • 1. Unicode Standardization: Normalizes text to Unicode NFC form, repairing corrupted diacritics and broken Meetei Mayek Cheikhei punctuation marks.
  • 2. Script Detection Regex: Uses Unicode range `[ꮀ-ꮿ]` to detect authentic Meetei Mayek script, discriminating it from Bengali-Assamese script (`[ঀ-৿]`) and English Latin characters.
  • 3. Bidirectional Transliteration: Converts between Meetei Mayek script, academic Latin transliteration, and phonemic English, enabling seamless cross-script matching.
  • 4. Alias Clustering: Groups disparate spelling variants (e.g., 'Cheitharol Kumbaba', 'Cheitharon Kumpapa', 'Royal Chronicle of Manipur') to a single canonical entity node.

SECTION 6: SEMANTIC VALIDATION & THE DIVERGENCE-FLAG PROTOCOL

The semantic validation layer represents the core intellectual and engineering contribution of this project, ensuring that low-resource historical memory is never homogenized.

6.1 Dense Entity Matching (validation/entity_matching.py)

To prevent entity duplication across multilingual mentions (e.g., matching English 'Kangla Sha' with Meetei Mayek 'ꯀꯪꯂꯥꯁꯥ'), the entity matcher builds an in-memory dense vector index:

  • 1. Embedding Model: Utilizes `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (384-dimensional dense vectors) for semantic text representation.
  • 2. Mathematical Matching Rule: Given mention $m$ with embedding $\mathbf{e}_m$ and candidate entity $k$ with embedding $\mathbf{e}_k$, similarity is computed via cosine distance:

$$\text{Sim}(m, k) = \frac{\mathbf{e}_m \cdot \mathbf{e}_k}{\|\mathbf{e}_m\| \|\mathbf{e}_k\|}$$

  • 3. Decision Boundary: If similarity is greater than or equal to 0.50, the mention is linked to entity k; otherwise, a new candidate entity node is flagged.

6.2 The Divergence-Flag Protocol (validation/divergence_detector.py)

When two sources make claims about the same `(entity_id, relation)` pair, the Divergence Detector evaluates their semantic distance:

$$D(c_1, c_2) = 1.0 - \frac{\mathbf{e}_{c_1} \cdot \mathbf{e}_{c_2}}{\|\mathbf{e}_{c_1}\| \|\mathbf{e}_{c_2}\|}$$

Divergence Grade Semantic Distance Metric (D) Operational Meaning System Action & Storage Behavior
NONE (Agreement) D < 0.20 Both sources state the same factual assertion Claims merged under single edge; citation list updated with combined sources.
SOFT (Variant) 0.20 <= D < 0.55 Differences in precision, counting, or spelling Merged with alternate labels; explanatory historiographical note recorded.
HARD (Conflict) D >= 0.55 Genuine factual, ideological, or chronological clash DO NOT MERGE. Reify both as competing E13 events in divergence group; display Dual-Perspective alert.

6.3 Historiographical Case Studies of Divergence

The system contains 23 hand-curated, verified historical divergence groups. Two representative examples illustrate the mechanism:

  • Case A: Dispute 1: Destruction of Kangla Sha Statues (1891): British colonial gazetteers state the sacred statues were 'demolished by colonial sappers as military reprisal'. In contrast, the royal chronicle Cheitharol Kumbaba and oral elders state the statues were 'dynamited under orders of General Collett to break the spiritual sovereignty of the Meetei nation'. The system preserves both accounts side-by-side with full citation.
  • Case B: Dispute 2: Burning of Sacred Puyas (Puya Meithaba, 1729 CE): State archives and British historical accounts describe the event as 'King Pamheiba adopting Ramanandi Hinduism and burning animist texts'. Indigenous puyas and oral elders emphasize that 'clandestine copies of sacred medical and cosmological texts were secretly buried and preserved across seven rural villages'. The engine surfaces both truths without erasing the oral memory.

SECTION 7: KNOWLEDGE GRAPH ENGINE & PROVENANCE SUBSYSTEM

The graph engine (kg/graph_engine.py) provides high-performance, in-memory graph operations backed by NetworkX, with an optional Neo4j Aura client connector.

7.1 NetworkX Property Graph Architecture

The primary engine utilizes a `networkx.MultiDiGraph`. Node and edge structures are formatted as follows:

  • 1. Node Storage: Each node key is a canonical entity ID (`E001`). Node attributes store `canonical_name`, `cidoc_class`, `entity_type`, `aliases`, `name_meitei_script`, `description`, `access_level`, and `media_pointers`.
  • 2. Directed Multigraph Edges: Edges represent semantic relations (e.g., `P108_was_produced_by`). Multiple edges between the same two nodes are supported, each carrying its unique `assignment_id`, `source_id`, `reliability_tier`, and `divergence_grade`.
  • 3. In-Memory Search Indexes: Fast inverted indexes maintain lowercase alias-to-ID mappings and Meetei Mayek script-to-ID mappings, providing O(1) lookup for common queries.

7.2 Subgraph Extraction Algorithm

When a user queries an entity, the graph engine performs an optimized Breadth-First Search (BFS) neighborhood extraction:

💻 SPECIFICATION / CODE: BFS Subgraph Extraction Algorithm
def get_subgraph(self, entity_id: str, hops: int = 1) -> Dict[str, Any]: if entity_id not in self.graph: return {"nodes": [], "edges": [], "divergence_flags": []} subgraph_nodes = set([entity_id]) frontier = set([entity_id]) for _ in range(hops): next_frontier = set() for node in frontier: neighbors = set(self.graph.successors(node)) | set(self.graph.predecessors(node)) next_frontier.update(neighbors) subgraph_nodes.update(next_frontier) frontier = next_frontier nodes_data = [] for nid in subgraph_nodes: entity = self.entities.get(nid) nodes_data.append({ "id": nid, "label": entity.canonical_name if entity else nid, "cidoc_class": entity.cidoc_class.value if entity else "Unknown", "script_name": entity.name_meitei_script if entity else None }) edges_data = [] for u in subgraph_nodes: for v in subgraph_nodes: if self.graph.has_edge(u, v): edge_dict = self.graph.get_edge_data(u, v) for edge_key, attrs in edge_dict.items(): edges_data.append({ "from": u, "to": v, "relation": attrs.get("relation", "P_related"), "divergence_grade": attrs.get("divergence_grade", "none") }) return {"nodes": nodes_data, "edges": edges_data}

SECTION 8: CROSSLINGUAL GRAPHRAG RETRIEVAL & GROUNDED SYNTHESIS

The GraphRAG pipeline coordinates query understanding, localized subgraph extraction, multimodal chunk retrieval, and evidence-grounded answer generation.

8.1 Query Translation & Intent Alignment (graphrag/query_translation.py)

When a query enters the system, it passes through the Query Translator:

  • 1. Script Detection: Checks for Meetei Mayek characters (`[ꮀ-ꮿ]`). If detected, translates key cultural concepts into canonical English equivalents using the `SCRIPT_CONCEPT_MAP` (e.g., 'ꯀꯪꯂꯥ' -> 'Kangla Fort', 'ꯄꯨꯌꯥ ꯃꯩ ꯊꯥꯕꯥ' -> 'Burning of Puyas').
  • 2. Entity Resolution: Identifies target entity IDs (`E001_KANGLA_FORT`) by checking canonical names, transliterations, and aliases.
  • 3. Relation Intent Extraction: Maps query question words to target ontological relations (e.g., 'Who built' -> `P108_was_produced_by`, 'When was' -> `P4_has_time-span`).

8.2 Dual-Stage Subgraph & Multimodal Chunk Retriever (graphrag/subgraph_retriever.py)

The retriever executes a two-stage extraction process:

  • 1. Stage 1 (Graph Traversal): Retrieves the entity's 1-hop neighborhood, isolating all active E13 attribute assignments, community annotations, and divergence flags.
  • 2. Stage 2 (Multimodal Bundle Assembly): Bundles matching media pointers: (a) manuscript folios from `data/manuscripts/`, (b) monument photographs from `data/images/`, and (c) audio recordings from `data/audio/`.

8.3 Grounded Answer Generation & Abstention (graphrag/answer_generator.py)

The answer generator converts the retrieved knowledge bundle into natural language while enforcing strict truth-grounding rules:

  • 1. Strict Attribution Rule: Every stated sentence must be followed by a source citation tag indicating reliability tier and source name (e.g., `[Royal Chronicle Cheitharol Kumbaba (Tier A)]`).
  • 2. Dual-Perspective Formatting: When a Hard Divergence (`divergence_grade == 'hard'`) is present, the generator formats a prominent Dual-Perspective Alert Card showing both the Colonial Archival Record and the Indigenous Oral Tradition side-by-side.
  • 3. Hallucination Abstention: If a query asks about an unrecorded historical fact (e.g., 'What was the secret password of the Kangla gate in 1500?'), the engine explicitly abstains: 'Historical records and community oral traditions do not contain documented evidence regarding this query.' It NEVER fabricates details.

SECTION 11: END-TO-END DATA FLOW & PROCESS TRACES

To understand how every internal process executes without visually inspecting the application, this section traces four complete real-world execution paths from user trigger to output.

11.1 Process Trace A: Executing a Cross-Lingual Query in Meetei Mayek Script

  • 1. Step 1 (User Input): The user inputs the query in indigenous Meetei Mayek script: 'ꯀꯪꯂꯥꯁꯥ ꯀꯅꯥꯅ ꯁꯥꯈꯤꯕꯒꯦ?' ('Who built the Kangla Sha statues?').
  • 2. Step 2 (Script Parsing & Normalization): TextNormalizer.detect_script() identifies Unicode range [\uAB80-\uABBF]. Normalizes text to NFC form.
  • 3. Step 3 (Concept Mapping): QueryTranslator.translate_query() queries SCRIPT_CONCEPT_MAP, identifying concept 'ꯀꯪꯂꯥꯁꯥ' -> 'Kangla Sha' and maps 'ꯁꯥꯈꯤꯕꯒꯦ' (built) to relation P108_was_produced_by.
  • 4. Step 4 (Entity Resolution): HeritageGraphEngine.find_entity('Kangla Sha') resolves canonical ID E002_KANGLA_SHA.
  • 5. Step 5 (Subgraph Retrieval): SubgraphRetriever.retrieve_bundle('E002_KANGLA_SHA') extracts all outgoing and incoming edges, attached E13 attribute assignments, divergence group D02, and media pointer kangla_sha_monument.jpg.
  • 6. Step 6 (Divergence Check & Lens Application): Checks active perspective lens. If 'Combined Lens' is selected and divergence grade is 'hard', retrieves both royal chronicle claims (King Khagemba foundation, King Bodhachandra restoration) and British colonial gazetteer claims.
  • 7. Step 7 (Grounded Answer Synthesis): AnswerGenerator.generate_answer() formats the response, attaches two source citation pills (Cheitharol Kumbaba [Tier A] and British Gazetteer [Tier C]), formats a Dual-Perspective Alert Card, and bundles the monument photograph.
  • 8. Step 8 (Client Render): The client receives the JSON payload, rendering the grounded text, citation pills, image card, and amber conflict card.

11.2 Process Trace B: Ingesting an Oral Recording under CARE Principles

  • 1. Step 1 (Audio File Arrival): WAV recording elder_kangla_testimony.wav is placed in data/audio/.
  • 2. Step 2 (Consent & Sovereignty Verification): SpeechASRModule._load_manifest() checks audio_manifest.csv. It verifies: speaker_consent=True, elder_role='Cultural Custodian', and access_tier='public'.
  • 3. Step 3 (Acoustic Processing & Transliteration): Generates spoken Manipuri transcript, Latin phonemic alignment, and English translation.
  • 4. Step 4 (Candidate Triple Mining): Extracts assertion: ('Kangla Fort', 'P53_has_former_or_current_location', 'Spiritual Navel of Kangleipak').
  • 5. Step 5 (Reification as E13 Event): Wraps claim into E13AttributeAssignment with evidence_modality=AUDIO_ASR, source_reliability_tier=TIER_A, and attaches the audio file pointer.
  • 6. Step 6 (Graph Ingestion): Injects edge into NetworkX MultiDiGraph, indexing the new claim under entity node E001_KANGLA_FORT.
